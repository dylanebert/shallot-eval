The cube now has diagonal amber and near-black stripes that slide across its surface all the time. The camera is where it was. I stopped the cube's old spin, so the only thing moving is the pattern.

**How it works**
- **`src/welcome/stripes.ts`** (new) defines a custom material called `StripeMaterial`, based on the scrolling checker in the installed `materials` example. The bands run along the line where x + y + z is the same, using the cube's own coordinates, so they carry on without a break from one face to the next. The light/dark split is a hard edge, so you see clear lines rather than a smooth gradient. A `time` value shifts the bands along that diagonal.
- **`src/welcome/index.ts`**: the cube uses `StripeMaterial` instead of the flat orange. The spin system is replaced by one that adds elapsed time to a clock every frame and passes it to the material. The ground is unchanged.
- To tweak the look, change `STRIPE_FREQUENCY` (default: 3 stripes per unit) and `STRIPE_SPEED` (default: 0.6 stripe widths per second) in `stripes.ts`. The colours are set where the cube's material is created in `index.ts`.

**Checks** (all pass)
- **`src/welcome/welcome.test.ts`** (updated): confirms the cube uses the stripe material and that the material's clock advances as time passes.
- **`tests/stripes.node.ts`** (new): renders the cube on the GPU with a camera that doesn't move. It found 204 dark and 193 light pixels in the middle of the cube, so both band colours are showing. After moving the pattern a quarter of a stripe width, 238 pixels had changed.
- `bun check`, `bun test` and the existing browser startup checks (`bunx playwright test`) pass. Those startup checks include the one that warns if the page starts drawing before the new material is ready.
